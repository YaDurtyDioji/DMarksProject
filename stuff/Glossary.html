Formulas

Earliest Finish (EF)
EF = ES + Duration
Calculated during the Forward Pass. The earliest time an activity can finish.
Latest Start (LS)
LS = LF - Duration
Calculated during the Backward Pass. The latest an activity can start without delaying the project.
Total Slack (Float)
Slack = LF - EF   OR   Slack = LS - ES
The amount of time an activity can be delayed without delaying the entire project. Critical path activities have zero slack.
Cost Performance Index (CPI)
CPI = CEV / CAC
Efficiency of cost use. Below 1.0 = over budget. Above 1.0 = under budget.
Cost Variance (CV)
CV = CEV - CAC
Negative = over budget. Positive = under budget. Zero = exactly on budget.
Forecasted Cost at Completion (FCAC/EAC)
FCAC = TBC / CPI
Forecasts total final cost assuming current cost efficiency continues.

Glossary of Terms

LU1: Introduction
Project

A temporary endeavor undertaken to create a unique product, service, or result. It has a defined beginning and end, distinguishing it from ongoing operations.

LU1: Introduction
Operations

Ongoing, repetitive activities that sustain the organization (e.g., daily customer service or factory production), as opposed to a temporary project.

LU1: Introduction
Triple Constraint

The traditional balance of Scope, Time, and Cost. A change in one constraint typically requires a change in the others to maintain project quality.

LU1: Introduction
Stakeholder

An individual, group, or organization that can affect, be affected by, or perceive itself to be affected by a project decision or outcome.

LU1: Introduction
Project Life Cycle

The four standard phases a project passes through: Initiating, Planning, Performing, and Closing.

PMBOK 7
Value (PMBOK 7)

The worth or usefulness of a project. PMBOK 7 emphasizes that completing a deliverable is not enough if it does not produce the intended benefit (outcome) that justified the project.

LU2: Selection
Project Charter

A high-level document issued by the sponsor that formally authorizes the project, commits funding, and outlines the objective, deliverables, risks, and milestone schedule.

LU2: Selection
Request for Proposal (RFP)

A formal document issued by a customer to potential contractors that defines project requirements and asks them to submit proposals (solutions, schedules, and pricing).

LU3: Proposals
Bid/No-Bid Decision

The contractor's assessment of whether responding to an RFP is strategically, financially, and operationally sensible before investing in a proposal.

LU3: Proposals
Fixed-Price Contract

A contract where the price is locked in. The contractor carries the risk of cost overruns. Best for well-defined projects with low uncertainty.

LU3: Proposals
Cost-Reimbursement Contract

A contract where the customer pays all allowable actual costs plus a fee. The customer carries the risk of cost overruns. Best for high-risk or uncertain projects (e.g., R&D).

LU4: Scope
Statement of Work (SOW)

A document that defines the major tasks and work the contractor must perform. It clarifies what is included in the scope to prevent misunderstandings.

LU4: Scope
SMART Objective

A project objective that is Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant/Realistic, and Time-bound.

LU4: Scope
Scope Creep

The uncontrolled expansion of project scope without adjusting the budget or schedule. Often occurs through small, unapproved additions by the customer.

LU4: Scope
Work Breakdown Structure (WBS)

A deliverable-oriented hierarchical decomposition of the total project scope. The lowest level is the Work Package, where costs and durations are estimated.

LU4: Scope
Responsibility Assignment Matrix (RAM)

A chart linking WBS items to team members. Uses "P" for Primary responsibility (the person in charge) and "S" for Support. Only one "P" per work package.

LU4: Sequence
Laddering

A scheduling technique for repetitive work (e.g., painting multiple rooms) where a following activity can begin on an earlier completed portion before the preceding activity has finished everywhere.

LU5: PMIS
Project Management Information System (PMIS)

Software tools (like MS Project, Jira, Asana) used to plan, schedule, track resources, and report on project progress. It automates complex recalculations.

LU6: Schedule
Critical Path

The longest-duration path through the network diagram. It determines the shortest possible project duration. Activities on this path have zero slack; any delay here delays the whole project.

LU6: Resources
Resource Leveling (Smoothing)

A technique used when the project deadline is fixed. The PM delays non-critical tasks (using their slack) to reduce peaks and fluctuations in resource demand, keeping resource usage even.

LU6: Resources
Resource-Limited Scheduling

A technique used when the quantity of available resources is fixed. If there aren't enough resources to do tasks in parallel, the project completion date will be extended to stay within limits.

LU7: Costs
Cumulative Earned Value (CEV)

The budgeted value of the work actually completed up to a specific point in time. (Also called EV or BCWP).

LU7: Costs
Cumulative Actual Cost (CAC)

The actual amount of money spent to perform the work up to a specific point in time.

LU8: Risk
Risk

An uncertain event or condition that, if it occurs, has a positive (opportunity) or negative (threat) effect on the project objectives.

LU8: Risk
Risk Avoidance

Changing the project plan or conditions so that the threat is completely eliminated (e.g., moving a construction site away from a flood zone).

LU8: Risk
Risk Mitigation

Taking action to reduce the likelihood and/or the impact of a threat (e.g., buying fire extinguishers).

LU8: Risk
Risk Assessment Matrix

A tool that combines the Likelihood (probability) of a risk and its Impact (severity) to help the PM prioritize which risks need immediate attention.

LU9: Closing
Lessons Learned

Knowledge gained during a project (both successes and mistakes) that is documented at closing to improve performance on future projects.

Agile
Agile Methodology

An iterative approach to project management (often software dev) that focuses on delivering working increments quickly, embracing changing requirements, and continuous customer feedback.

Agile
Agile Principles

Core values from the Agile Manifesto. Key examples: 1. Satisfy the customer through early delivery. 2. Welcome changing requirements. 3. Deliver working software frequently. 4. Business people and developers work together daily.

Agile
Scrum Master

A servant-leader role in Scrum. They do not manage the team; instead, they remove obstacles (impediments) and facilitate Scrum ceremonies to ensure the team can perform effectively.

Agile
Sprint (Iteration)

A short, fixed timebox (usually 1-4 weeks) in Scrum during which a specific set of features from the Product Backlog is developed and made ready for review.

Agile
Product Backlog

An ordered, prioritized list of all requirements, features, and user stories for a product, maintained by the Product Owner.

Agile
Kanban

A visual Agile framework (using boards and cards) that helps teams visualize workflow, limit Work-In-Progress (WIP), and maximize efficiency. It does not require fixed-length sprints like Scrum.

Org Structure
Functional Organizational Structure

Groups individuals by discipline (e.g., Marketing, Engineering). Best for standard products. The PM has limited authority, and communication flows up/down the functional hierarchy.

Org Structure
Autonomous (Projectized) Organizational Structure

Each project operates as its own independent entity with dedicated full-time resources. The PM has full authority. Highly responsive to customers, but can be cost-inefficient.

Org Structure
Matrix Organizational Structure

A hybrid of functional and projectized. Team members report to both a functional manager (home department) and a project manager. Balances resource efficiency with project focus.

Org Structure
Project Management Office (PMO)

An organizational unit that standardizes project management processes, provides templates/training, and oversees multiple projects to ensure strategic alignment.
